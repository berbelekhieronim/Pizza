Exploring patterns

60 pizzas. Screening: the best grouping (Any neurodivergence (yes / no)) has 19 in its smaller group, enough for a strong difference to pass the correction over this page's tests. A large effect needs about 24 per group for 80% power.

For exploring, not confirming. First the ideas worth testing, then the best-supported leads, then each grouping in full (108 measures, 49 of them per topping). Effects are Cliff's δ (−1 to 1: how often a value from the group is higher than one from the comparison, minus how often lower; for yes/no measures, the difference in percentages); p from a Mann-Whitney test (by permutation under 8 per group); q the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate over a family of questions (the study's groupings with the self-reports; demographics apart; the ideas apart); bootstrap 95% intervals for groups of 5 or more.

Ideas to test

Leads worth checking, from what is known about eating in autism and ADHD (docs/sample-sizes.md). They are not hypotheses: nothing here is preregistered, and some have no expected direction. They are tested as one family of 42 tests (39 can run on these data), so q is corrected over these only, not over the whole screen below: this is where a real difference can show first. A test is a strong lead when q < 0.10 and, where a direction is expected, it goes that way. Every test adds to the correction, so each idea shows how many tests it runs and how many people that takes. To confirm, pick 3-5 ideas, state their direction in a preregistration, and test them in a new sample. How every sample size here is worked out, with the formulas and worked examples: Methods → Sample sizes (hover a number for the formula).
